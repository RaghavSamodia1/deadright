How it was designed

Rules the build had to keep.

Each one is checkable against the codebase, not a poster on a wall.

No nav bar.

The home screen is the navigation. A tab bar spends a permanent strip of every screen on four fixed guesses about where you want to go.

15 destinations, one tap from the front door

Bento sized by weight.

A tile’s area is its importance. The sizes compose — a hero is exactly two stat tiles plus the gap — so the grid never needs a number nobody can justify.

4 tile sizes, derived not guessed

Fewer taps, or it doesn’t ship.

Resolving used to need both people to agree before anything moved. Now one person calls it and it settles at once; the other side gets 24 hours to object.

A two-party handshake cut to one tap

Colour is state, never decoration.

Amber wants something from you. Mint is a win. Coral is contested. If a card is grey, nothing is being asked.

39 tokens, every hue load-bearing

A badge means it needs you.

Settled bets drop to plain text, so the loudest thing in the feed is always the thing to act on — never the thing already finished.

Won and Lost carry no pill

The guest needs no account.

Party pools are built for people who will never install the app. Scan, pick, done — no download, no signup, no password to forget.

0 taps of onboarding to vote

Errors are sentences.

A database saying not_participant under a submit button is the app blaming you in its own language. Every raised code became English.

21 codes mapped

Motion has to mean something.

A stagger gives nine tiles a reading order. Numbers count up because arriving at a figure differs from being handed one. Only a countdown under five minutes pulses.
