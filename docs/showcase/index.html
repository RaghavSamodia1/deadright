Sticky stack

Rules the build had to keep.

01

Pinned, never hijacked.

Nothing here intercepts your wheel or animates the page for you. Every act is a tall track with a position:sticky child, so the browser pins natively at compositor speed. Scroll is only ever read. Momentum, Page Down, find-in-page and screen readers all behave exactly as they would on a plain document.

02

One engine, one frame.

Five techniques share a single requestAnimationFrame loop and one scroll read per frame. Each act registers a start and end offset and is handed its own 0–1. Nothing can drift out of sync with anything else, and there is never more than one loop running.

03

Transform and opacity only.

Twenty cards and a phone move every frame, so nothing may touch layout. Geometry is measured once, cached, and recomputed only on resize; the loop writes translate3d, rotate, scale and opacity and reads nothing back. An earlier version animated a blur radius and locked the renderer up outright.

04

Damped, then stopped.

Raw scroll is jittery on a trackpad and steppy on a wheel, so the rendered value chases the real one by 12% a frame — that is where the weight comes from. When the gap closes it snaps to exact and the loop ends rather than idling at 60fps against a still page.
